# Changelog

All notable changes to Hoard are recorded here. The format follows
[Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and Hoard uses
[semantic versioning](https://semver.org/).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **Search all of Hoard from the top of every page,** or press Ctrl K (⌘ K on a Mac). Find materials, bases, products, lots, production runs, purchases, orders, stocktakes and holds by name, notes and text properties, or by any code on them: SKUs, supplier codes, lot numbers, run, purchase and order codes, and a store's order and customer numbers. Codes match however they're written (`LOT/24/07` finds `lot 2407`), and accents don't matter. Suggestions show as you type; scan a label and press Enter to go straight to its record. The results page has a tab for each kind of record, can include archived ones, and offers close matches when nothing matches. Costs and prices are never searched.
- **Units that convert.** Hoard knows the standard units of weight, volume (US), length and count, from milligrams to gallons, and converts between those that measure the same thing. Every unit field is a search: type "g", "grams" or "kilo" to find it, and an admin can add a unit of your own, such as a jar or a bag, right there. Standard units can't be renamed or removed; Settings › Units lists them beside your own.
- **Purchase conversions fill themselves in.** A material bought in kilograms and tracked in grams converts at 1000 without anyone typing it. A unit of your own still takes the number you type.
- **Recipes by ratio.** Write a recipe by amount, as before, or by ratio: each ingredient's parts and the batch's ingredients total, with what the batch makes kept separately for loss. A line can be written in any unit that converts to its ingredient's, such as 0.5 kg of something tracked in grams. Lines measured differently from the total, such as a flavoring in mL, scale along with the rest. The recipe shows each line's ratio and amount side by side, switches between the two ways of writing it, and **Scale to** can now scale to an amount of one ingredient.
- **Make as much as one ingredient allows.** A new run can use up an amount of one ingredient, by default all that's available at the run's location, leaving out expired lots. It says which other ingredients are short and the most you can make with what's there. A run's page shows short lines until it's completed, and says when its recipe has changed since it was planned.
- **What one unit weighs,** recorded on a material not tracked by weight, so declarations can weigh it.
- **Receive part of a purchase line.** When 200 g of a 453.592 g line arrives, receive just that: it gets its own lot, and the rest stays on order at the same unit cost. The purchase's discount and shipping stay spread as they were. Taking the part back puts it back into the rest while the rest is still on order.
- **Purchases record when they're expected,** and an open purchase past that day is marked overdue on its page and in the purchases list.
- **Notes on a purchase,** which search finds. Purchase lines also show each material's or product's SKU and category.
- **Archive a base or a material,** as you can a product. It leaves the lists, the item pickers and search until you ask for archived ones, and stops warning of low stock. Its stock still counts in stock value, cost of goods and stocktakes, and recipes and open runs that use it keep working, marked archived. Unarchive it and it's back as it was.
- **Bins.** Record where a base or a material sits at each location, by zone, section and position, from its stock table. A bin stays when the stock there runs out, and two items can share one.
- **A picture and notes on a base or a material.** Search finds the notes. The picture stays in Hoard and is never sent to a store.
- **Start a new base or material with its stock.** Adding one takes its picture, its bin and its starting stock at a location, the main one unless you pick another, with what each unit cost for members who see costs.
- **A method on a recipe,** shown above its lines.
- **A labour rate** in Settings, what an hour of work costs, which an admin sets and only members who see costs read.
- **A base's potential, at a glance:** how much the stock you have could make, beside how much its runs have made and when a stocktake last counted it. A run draws from one location, so potential adds up what each location could make: catnip in one place and chamomile in another make nothing until one is moved. It leaves out what open orders have committed, lots on hold and expired lots, just as **Make as much as** does, so the two always agree. The stock table shows what could be made and the last stocktake at each location.
- **The recipe, costed.** Each line shows what's on hand and, to members who see costs, what it costs; then labour at the labour rate, the batch's cost and the manufacture cost of one unit. An ingredient that's run out is costed at what it last cost, marked as such, and one that has never had a cost is flagged, with the batch's cost marked incomplete.
- **A base's and a material's history** on its page: the production runs that made it and those that used it, its adjustments, and the stocktakes that counted it, expected against counted, a page at a time.
- **Types of material, base and product, each with its own page.** A type decides which fields its records' page shows and where, never what a record does: a field its page hides still counts, and changing a record's type, or a type's page, clears nothing. Each type's page starts from a shape Hoard ships, until an admin lays it out: **Ingredient** materials show their ingredient name, allergens, fragrance allergens, the weight of one unit and their declarations, and **Packaging** hides those; **Made** products show their recipe and what it declares, **Bought** ones hide the recipe, and a **Bundle** shows what it holds without stock or lots. Admins keep the types in Settings › Types, beside categories; each kind has a default type, which stays, and a type records use stays until they're moved to another. Lists show each record's type and filter by it, and search shows it beside each result. Moving a record to a type whose page requires something it lacks asks for it first.
- **A category can name a default type,** which a record made in it takes unless you choose another, or an admin makes a new one right there in the type picker. A category chosen later, or a category's new default type, changes no record's type.
- **Buy finished products.** A purchase line can name a product's variation as well as a material: receiving it puts the stock into that variation as a lot, at the line's landed cost, received in part or whole and on its own day as materials are. A bought product gets a real average cost, and one that's both bought and made averages both. Receiving a product a store lists sends its new count to the store, at each location the store sells from. A bundle isn't bought: buy what it holds.
- **A variation records how it's bought:** its preferred supplier, their code for it, and the unit it comes in. A variation counts in ones, so a dozen converts at 12 by itself, and a unit of your own, such as a box of 50, takes the number you type. Purchase lines use it to work out how many arrive.
- **A product without a recipe declares what it records itself:** its ingredient name, allergens and fragrance allergens, as a material does, so a bought snack declares, and so does a gift box holding it. A product's page shows them once its type's page is laid out to.
- **Recipes can hold products.** A gift box can be made of an infuser, a tea tin and a box, with the infuser still sold on its own from the same stock. A product line is written in each or dozen, scales along in a ratio recipe like a flavoring in mL, and counts as unweighed in the shares by weight; its declarations come through its own recipe, or what a bought one records. A run that uses a product a store lists sends its new count to the store, and open orders it leaves short say so. A bundle's variation can't be a line, since a bundle has no stock of its own, and nothing can end up made of itself. Auto manufacture still makes only what was sold: a gift box sold with no infusers takes them below zero rather than making them too.
- **What's on order, on a material's page:** what its purchases still to arrive will bring, in its tracking unit, marked overdue when one of them is late.
- **Usage and purchases, on a material's and a base's page:** how much arrived by purchase and how much runs used over the last week, month, 3, 6 and 12 months, and all time, each on the day it happened. Losses, corrections and stocktakes count as neither.
- **Used in, on a material's and a base's page:** every recipe that uses it, how much one batch takes in the unit it's written in, and what the recipe makes, marked when that's archived.
- **A product at a glance:** its SKU under its name, and tiles for what's on hand, committed to open orders, available, what its recipe could make and what's on order, then what it has made, sold (less what was refunded before it shipped) and shipped (less returns), its variations added up. A bundle has no stock of its own: what's available is how many its contents make together.
- **Profitability, on a product's page,** to members who see costs: revenue from its order lines after their discounts, less refunds and returns, without shipping or tax; what what shipped cost when it went out; and the gross profit with its share of revenue. An order counts once it ships, a bundle's sale counts its contents' cost, and a shipment that went out with no cost is flagged. A store's orders keep their currency, and orders in another currency than yours are left out and counted.
- **Price points,** such as Retail and Wholesale, which an admin who sees costs keeps in Settings: each with a markup on cost, a listing fee per unit sold and a commission on the price. Hoard starts with none.
- **Pricing, on a product's page,** to members who see costs: each variation's cost, by its materials (or contents), labour at the labour rate and overhead; and at each price point, its price, typed or left to the price suggested from its cost and rounded to your currency's smallest unit, with the fee, the commission and the margin left. A margin below cost is marked, and a variation that has never had a cost shows its suggested price and margin as unknown.
- **Overhead** in Settings: a percentage added to what goods and labour cost, which suggested prices and margins count.
- **A customer's price point.** Pick the price point a customer buys at when you make an order for them, and new lines start at its prices: its price there, else the variation's own price, else the product's default price. You can still change a line. Orders from a store keep the store's prices, and a price point is never sent to a store. Remove a price point and its customers' orders keep their prices, and new ones start at the default.
- **A product's history** on its page: the runs that made it, its orders, its purchases, its adjustments and the stocktakes that counted it, a variation at a time; and its usage and the recipes using it.
- **Move stock between locations.** **Move stock** on an item's stock table moves a lot, or stock in no lot, from one location to another as one record, at its average cost, which the move leaves as it was even when all of it moves. The lot keeps its number, properties, documents and hold, a cure included, at the other location, and moving it back puts it back into the same lot. A move counts as neither received nor lost in cost of goods and the mass balance, and it sends the new count to a store at each end the store sells from. Open orders left short at the old location say so. Moves are listed with the adjustments, and a move is undone by moving the stock back. Traces and recalls follow stock to where it was moved.
- **Consignment.** In Settings › Locations, an admin marks a location with a contact as a consignee's: a shop holding your stock to sell. Stock moved there is consigned. It counts as on hand, in stock value, cost of goods and stocktakes, and a product's figures and every stock table show it, but it isn't available to orders elsewhere, to **Make as much as** or to potential, and it's never sent to a store, so a consignee's location can't be mapped to a store's. When the shop reports what sold, enter an order for its contact shipped from its location. A recall names the consignee's location where recalled stock went.
- **Call recipes and bases your own words.** An admin names them in Settings › Words, such as "formula" or "bill of materials" for a recipe and "blend" or "sub-assembly" for a base, and every page says it, as typed. Leave one empty and Hoard says "recipe" or "base" in your language.
- **A crafts and assembly starting setup,** for jewellery, pins, games and merch: it calls recipes bills of materials and bases sub-assemblies, and brings Jewellery, Pins and Games as made products, Merch as bought, gift sets as a bundle, Components and Packaging as material types, and categories, units, properties and waste reasons of its own.
- **A production run's page shows the whole batch:** its figures, with labour cost beside materials cost; each ingredient's picture, SKU and cost per unit; its own notes beside its recipe's method; linked Initiative files; the order auto-manufacture made it for; and every order that shipped its lot.

### Changed

- **A purchase line is received on the day it arrived,** today unless you pick another, and its lot and stock take that date rather than the purchase's. Change the day later and costs follow it. A line can't arrive before its purchase's date. Lines received before this update keep their purchase's date, so nothing already recorded moves.
- **Your units become Hoard's standard units.** A unit named like a standard one ("g", "grams", "Kg") becomes that unit, and two of yours that are the same unit become one. A material bought and tracked in standard units, such as kilograms and grams, takes their real conversion in place of the number typed in; purchases already received keep what they recorded.
- **A tracking unit can't change once something is counted in it:** stock movements, purchases, stocktakes, recipes or runs. Before then it changes freely.
- Starting setups no longer bring the standard units, which every community has.
- **Every starting setup brings types.** Each adds Packaging for materials, its kinds of base where it has more than one, such as Oil blend and Fragrance blend, bought products, and a gift box or set as a bundle, with its categories defaulting to them. Cosmetics and soap calls recipes formulas.
- **Every material, base and product takes its kind's default type,** and a page an admin laid out before this update becomes that type's page, so nothing looks different until you add types.
- **Archived products leave the products list and the item pickers** until you ask for archived ones, as archived bases and materials do. Recipes, bundles, orders and runs that use one keep working.

- **Hoard is in beta.** On a deployment that supports it, the platform owner picks the communities that may install Hoard.

- **Hoard follows its own state in your community.** When Initiative sets Hoard read-only, on hold or deleted in a community, Hoard stops its own work there (pushing stock to stores, updating linked tasks and running background jobs) and changes nothing, while everything it holds is kept and stays readable. It picks up where it left off once Hoard is active again. Hoard deletes nothing in any state.

### Fixed

- **Ingredient lists and fragrance allergens weigh what they list.** A material tracked in kilograms now outweighs one tracked in grams as it should, and one tracked by volume or by count is no longer counted as that many grams: it's listed as unweighed, at the end, and its fragrance shares are marked incomplete, until you record what one of its units weighs.

## [0.2.0] - 2026-11-15

### Added

- **Sync with Shopify.** A newly connected store waits, untouched, until an admin chooses **Sync with Shopify** in Settings › Stores. A short guide explains what syncing does and lets you choose which Hoard location each store location's stock goes into. The sync then reads the store's products, its last 60 days of orders and its stock and costs into Hoard: matched products take the store's names and SKUs, the others are made in Hoard, and Hoard's counts are set to the store's. Nothing is sent to the store, and the sync carries on if you leave the page, with its progress shown in Settings. **Remove sync** stops it and keeps everything it brought in.
- **Hoard keeps a copy of each store product,** up to date as it changes in the store, so a product's store details show without asking the store each time. Products made in the store after the sync are made in Hoard too; one deleted in the store keeps its Hoard product.
- **A Production planning dashboard** in the marketplace, with five new widgets. **What to make next** turns recent sales (and, once Hoard holds a year of orders, the same weeks last year) into whole batches to make, after what's available, curing and already planned, with the hours they take, and lists the bases those runs need more of. **Materials to order** shows what the runs ahead need beyond what's in stock and on order, and the day to order it by from how long the supplier usually takes. **Production hours ahead** shows the hours of work in each of the next four weeks, **Cost to make** each product's unit cost over its last two runs beside the store's price and margin, for members who see costs, and **Expiring soon** the lots expiring within 30 days.
- **Sales by week,** a widget to add to any dashboard: the units of each product sold each week over the last 12 weeks, without refunds or cancelled orders.
- **Shopify's fields line up with Hoard's.** The sync guide lists each field your store defines for its products and variants, with how many products use it, and suggests what it fills in Hoard: your allergens or ingredient list, a product property with the same name, or a new property of the right kind (category fields that pick from a list become a choice of the same entries). Change any of them, pair allergen values Hoard doesn't recognise, or leave a field out; change them later under Settings › Stores › Show the fields. Properties stay in step both ways: an edit in Hoard is saved to Shopify first, and a change in Shopify comes back into Hoard. Allergens and ingredient lists come from your recipes, so the sync only compares them: where Hoard's differ, Settings › Stores lists the products, and nothing is sent until you choose **Send Hoard's values to Shopify**. After that, a recipe change that changes them is sent to Shopify. Hoard now asks the store's app for the `read_metaobjects` permission.
- **Customer numbers on store orders.** Each Shopify order keeps the store's number for its customer, and nothing else about them. An order's page lists the customer's other orders, and the orders list filters by customer number. Hoard now asks the store's app for the `read_customers` permission.

### Changed

- **A synced store's orders are managed in the store.** Hoard shows them as they stand, with **Open in Shopify** to change them there; you still record which lot each shipment draws and what comes back. Orders you make by hand, and a store's orders while it isn't synced, change in Hoard as before.
- **Editing a store's product in Hoard writes to the store.** A new name, status, SKU or option on a product a synced store lists is saved to the store first, and archiving it archives it there. If the product changed in the store since you opened it, nothing is saved and Hoard shows the store's newer version. A product the store lists can't be deleted in Hoard: archive it, or delete it in the store first.
- A store's locations start with no Hoard location: the sync, or an admin, chooses where each one's stock goes.
- Exports run in the background: Hoard shows each one's progress, then offers its file to download once it's ready. Only the member who started an export can download it, for a week.
- Hoard's pages no longer repeat the tab's name as a heading above their own tabs.

### Fixed

- Changing a product's auto-manufacture setting no longer clears its cure time and its traceability flags.
- **Refunds and edits in your store reach Hoard.** A line refunded or removed before it ships stops holding stock, and the store is sent the new count; one refunded after it shipped stays shipped, and stock comes back when you record the return. An order's page shows what was ordered, what it sells now, and what was refunded.
- **Changes to orders older than 60 days are kept.** Shopify stops answering for an order after 60 days, so Hoard now keeps the change its webhook describes.
- **An older copy of an order never replaces a newer one,** when the store's webhooks arrive out of order.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-11-13

### Added

- **Bases and recipes.** A base is something you make to use in other things, like a spice blend or cold brew. Recipes hold materials and bases, nest as deep as you need, scale to any batch, and show each line's share by weight. A bundle, such as a gift box, holds the products inside it.
- **Production runs.** Plan a run from its recipe, start it, and complete it with what you actually made: the materials it used come out of stock, and what it made goes in as a lot named for the run. Lots you don't pick are taken by their category's picking rule. Reopen a run to take it back. A run is marked uncosted when a material it used had no cost.
- **Runs and purchases on your project's tasks.** A task can track a run or a purchase and show its status, and **Complete run** and **Received in full** act on it from the task. Automations can make and link those tasks, and start flows when a run starts or finishes, a purchase is placed or received, or stock runs low.
- **Ingredients and allergens.** Mark each material's allergens, or a delivered lot's own. Products and bases roll them up through their recipes, a run's lots show what went in, and ingredients list heaviest first. A material nobody has checked shows as undeclared.
- **Trace any lot back to its suppliers and forward to where it went.** Each lot has a page of its own: what went into it, down to the purchases and suppliers it came from, and everything made from it and every order that shipped it, step by step. Sales that named no lot show where it may have gone.
- **Recall in one action.** Recall a lot, or every lot of an item dated in a range, to see everything made from it and every shipment it reached, then download the FDA's sortable spreadsheet, Canada's plain-text file or a PDF.
- **Holds.** Put a lot on hold with a reason (curing, awaiting test results, suspect, recall or your own) and the date it's ready on. Held stock stays out of picking, runs and shipments and isn't counted as available, so your store and your orders don't sell it, and an order it leaves short shows as short. Release a hold once it's ready, or discard some or all of the lot. A run of something with a cure time puts what it made on hold to cure, and an order can put every lot it drew on hold, with everything made from them. A widget lists the holds ready to check.
- **Picking rules per category.** Each category says which lot goes first: earliest expiry, first in, last in, or always recorded, where every run and shipment names its lot.
- **Link Initiative files to lots, products, materials and bases,** such as certificates and test reports. Several on one lot list together, and each person sees only the files they can open in Initiative.
- **EU fragrance allergens.** Record each fragrance allergen's share in a material or a delivered lot. Products and lots list the ones past the threshold to declare, which is lower for a product left on the skin than for one rinsed off, and name the materials nobody has checked.
- **How long to keep your records.** Tick the rules you keep records for in Settings, and Hoard shows how long your backups need to go back.
- **Choose your kind of business when you first open Hoard.** Admins pick it from a list, and Hoard starts with the units, categories, lot details and waste reasons it uses; "Something else" starts blank. Tea, coffee and drinks adds waste by reason, planned against actual, and an allergen matrix to Reports; Bakery and food, Cosmetics and soap, and Restaurant, café and bar come with their own picking rules and hold reasons.
- **Stock, Production and Reports pages in each initiative Hoard is placed in, and Settings for community admins.** Hoard keeps one set of stock for the whole community, whichever initiative it's opened from.
- **Materials, products and variations,** with SKUs, categories, purchase and tracking units, low-stock limits and product pictures. Each record page shows stock and lots per location, and who added and last changed it.
- **Purchases from your community's contacts.** Receiving a line makes its lot and moves the material's average cost. Discount and shipping are spread over the lines by value, and tax is kept out. Add a new supplier from the picker without leaving the purchase.
- **Orders and shipments.** Take orders from your business customers, picked from your community's contacts, on an Orders page of their own. Send an order in one shipment or several, with its carrier and tracking number; each line leaves the lot you pick, or the one that expires first. Record what comes back: restocked into its lot, or written off as returned damaged.
- **Connect your Shopify store.** The store's owner makes an app for their store in Shopify, and a community admin saves its details under Hoard's connections in Initiative. Hoard checks the store as it connects, and Settings says why when it can't.
- **Your store's products, matched and edited live.** Each store product is linked to what it sells in Hoard by SKU, then title, or by hand. Edit its title, description, status and images from Hoard straight into the store, or add a product to the store as a draft. Hoard tells the store what is available at each location you map, keeps it in step as stock moves, and keeps a log of what it sent.
- **Store orders copied in, and made to order as they sell.** Paid orders hold their stock, bundles split into their parts, and the store's fulfillments become shipments with their lots. Connecting brings in the last 60 days of orders without moving stock, except those still waiting to be sent. Auto-manufacture makes what a paid order sells from its recipe, such as drinks made fresh: always, or only when it is out of stock, for every product or one at a time.
- **Cost of goods, mass balance and certified volume.** Three reports: what the goods cost over a period, as a tax return asks for it; what each item had, took in, made, used, sold and lost, split by a lot property if you like; and how much certified stock, such as fair trade, came in against how much was sold.
- **Adjustments and stocktakes.** Record a change with a reason, or count materials or products (all, a sample, or one category) and confirm each row. Each stocktake shows its accuracy.
- **Reports:** what to reorder and what your stock is worth, plus CSV and JSON exports of every record.
- **Widgets** for stock to reorder, stock value, waste by reason, planned against actual and the allergen matrix, and a **Stock overview dashboard.**
- **Your own properties and layouts.** Admins add properties to materials, products and lots, and choose which fields each list and record page shows. Everyone fills them in.
- **Settings** for units, categories, locations and the contact holding each one's address, adjustment and hold reasons, and your currency.
- **"See costs and prices",** a permission your community's roles hold. Without it, costs and prices stay hidden.
